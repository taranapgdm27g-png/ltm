About me

Why I chose English first, and finance next

I have always been a go-getter who keeps learning. At school I was a subject topper with equally strong marks across every subject, and finished with 90.8% in Class XII and 89.2% in Class X.

B.Com (Hons) was the obvious next step. I chose English Honours at Delhi University instead, because I wanted a more varied lens and to understand the world from other people's perspectives. Those three years taught me to structure my thinking, tell a clear story with evidence, manage stakeholders and lead a room; they also cured my stage fright.

An MBA was always the final step, for the financial literacy to go with it. My PGDM in Finance and Analytics at Great Lakes Gurgaon gives me that, and at Paytm the two came together: the numbers from finance, the people and the writing from English.

Valuation and due diligence

Larsen & Toubro, read from its filings

Valuation for the FSAV course, team of four; diligence memo written on my own, October 2026

What I did: rebuilt L&T's audited FY25 and FY26 statements, analysed sixteen ratios and a five-year DuPont breakdown, valued the business with a ten-year DCF and peer multiples, then wrote a three-page memo on what the headline numbers leave out.

12.8%WACC, Blume-adjusted beta 1.13
11.0% to 4.1%net working capital to sales
+21%contract and other current assets vs 11.8% revenue growth
8red flags ranked, 10 questions for management
Read the findings

FY26 profit rose 7% (18% recurring), but other income and lower interest supplied 46% of the rise in pre-exceptional profit, and 35% of operating cash flow before lending came from a working-capital release. If net working capital returned to 8% of core revenue, the outflow would be about ₹10,500 crore. The balance sheet is strong; the risks are earnings quality and reversibility, not solvency. At a 12.8% cost of capital the base DCF gives ₹1,772 a share against ₹3,837 in the market, which prices a cost of capital nearer 9 to 10% and a ₹7.4 lakh crore order book.
